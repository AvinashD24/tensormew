# RL (and RL + LLM) for beating video games: a verified history, as background for a full Pokémon Crystal paper

Compiled 2026-10-19 with WebSearch/WebFetch. Companion notes: `llm-agents-pokemon.md` (Pokémon LLM agents in depth), `rl-techniques-long-horizon.md` (technique catalogue), `../papers/neurips2025-pokemon-red-rl.md` (Pleines et al. / pokerl / PokeAgent reading note).

**Verification legend**
- **[V]** = title, year, and venue (and the one or two facts quoted from the abstract) were confirmed by a search/fetch this session.
- **[M]** = from memory, canonical citation, not re-checked this session. Treat the venue/year as probably right and every number as a lead.

## 0. One-paragraph takeaway

"Beating a game" has meant at least six different things (score threshold, defeating humans, milestone in a tech tree, reaching the credits, speedrunning, held-out generalization). The games that fell to RL did so with some mix of: a huge amount of simulated experience, self-play against a population, hand-shaped reward, an imitation prior from human data, or the ability to reset the emulator to arbitrary states. Long-horizon story RPGs sit at the hard end of this space: sparse milestones spread over >10^5-10^6 decisions, heavy partial observability, hierarchical gating (items, HMs, flags), and no score. The best Pokémon results so far are either LLM harnesses that read RAM-derived text (community-reported, not peer-reviewed full-game results), or RL projects that lean on scripts and shaping (pokerl), or short-scope academic baselines (Pleines et al., to Cerulean City). No peer-reviewed, scripts-off, end-to-end RL clear of a Pokémon game exists that I could find [absence of evidence only; UNVERIFIED].

## 1. Chronological survey

### 1.1 Atari: from DQN to Agent57 (2013-2020)

- **DQN** - Mnih et al., "Human-level control through deep reinforcement learning," *Nature* 518, 2015 [M]. Beat: human-level on ~half of 49 Atari games from pixels. Algorithm: Q-learning + conv net + experience replay + target network. Compute: ~50M frames per game [M]. Human data/scripts: none; reward clipping is a mild form of shaping. Lesson: end-to-end deep RL from pixels works, but Montezuma's Revenge scored ~0, which defined the next five years of exploration work.
- **Rainbow** - Hessel et al., AAAI 2018 [M]. Combines six DQN extensions (double Q, prioritized replay, dueling, multi-step, distributional, noisy nets). ~200M frames per game; the MuZero blog quotes Rainbow at ~231% median human-normalized and about 10 days of training [V, secondary via search result]. Lesson: the gains came from engineering and ablation of known tricks rather than new ideas.
- **Ape-X (Horgan et al., ICLR 2018) and R2D2 (Kapturowski et al., ICLR 2019)** [M]. Distributed prioritized replay with hundreds of CPU actors and one GPU learner; R2D2 adds recurrent state storage plus burn-in so partial observability can be handled with an LSTM. No human data. Lesson: scale plus recurrence is what later agents (NGU, Agent57) are built on; R2D2's stored-state/burn-in recipe is directly relevant to any recurrent policy for a partially observable RPG.
- **Agent57** - Badia et al., "Agent57: Outperforming the Atari Human Benchmark," ICML 2020 [V]. First deep RL agent above the standard human baseline on all 57 Atari games [V]. Algorithm: NGU-style family of policies from exploratory to exploitative, a bandit meta-controller choosing among them, plus a new value-function parameterization for stability [V]. Compute: tens of billions of frames for the hardest games [M, UNVERIFIED exact number]. Human data: none. Lesson: no single exploration/exploitation setting works across games; adapt it online.
- **MuZero** - Schrittwieser et al., "Mastering Atari, Go, chess and shogi by planning with a learned model," *Nature* 588, 2020 [V]. Tree search over a learned model (predicts policy, value, reward) set a new state of the art on 57 Atari games and matched AlphaZero on board games without being given rules [V]. The Reanalyze variant reportedly reached 731% median human-normalized in about 12 hours, vs ~10 days for Rainbow [V, secondary]. Lesson: learned-model planning is sample-efficient where the dynamics are learnable.

### 1.2 Hard exploration: Montezuma's Revenge, Pitfall (2016-2021)

- **Demonstration-based and reset-based shortcuts (2018).** Salimans & Chen, "Learning Montezuma's Revenge from a Single Demonstration," arXiv 1812.03381 [V]: PPO where each episode starts from a state along one human demonstration, moving the start point backwards as the agent masters each stage; score 74,500, a then-best published result [V]. Aytar et al., "Playing hard exploration games by watching YouTube," NeurIPS 2018 [M, not re-checked]. Lesson: if you can reset the simulator to a good state, "exploration" becomes a series of short, easy problems. This is directly applicable to PyBoy save states.
- **RND** - Burda et al., "Exploration by Random Network Distillation," ICLR 2019 [V]. Bonus = error predicting the output of a fixed random network; PPO with two value heads for intrinsic and extrinsic reward. First method to beat the average human on Montezuma's Revenge without demonstrations or access to underlying state; occasionally clears level 1 [V]. Lesson: cheap novelty bonuses work, but only up to a point (single-room-scale credit assignment).
- **NGU** - Badia et al., "Never Give Up: Learning Directed Exploration Strategies," ICLR 2020 [V]. Episodic k-NN novelty (embedding trained with an inverse-dynamics model so it tracks controllable state) multiplied by a lifelong RND term; one network learns many exploration/exploitation trade-offs via UVFA [V]. Lesson: per-episode novelty is the right complement to lifelong novelty. Pokémon relevance: "visit a new map section this episode" vs "never revisit Route 29".
- **Go-Explore** - Ecoffet, Huizinga, Lehman, Stanley, Clune. arXiv 1901.10995 (2019) as "Go-Explore: a New Approach for Hard-Exploration Problems" [V]; published as "First return, then explore," *Nature* 590, 580-586, Feb 2021 [V via IDEAS/RePEc and sotaverified]. The arXiv abstract reports >43,000 points on Montezuma's Revenge (about 4x the prior state of the art), >650,000 average (best run ~18M) when human domain knowledge defines the cell representation, and the first positive score on Pitfall (~60,000 average with domain knowledge) [V, arXiv v4 abstract]. The Nature paper additionally reports all unsolved Atari games solved and a sparse-reward pick-and-place robotics task [V, via secondary summary]. Algorithm: archive of "cells"; phase 1 returns to a cell by restoring a simulator state (or replaying), then explores; phase 2 robustifies the best trajectory into a policy with imitation / the backward algorithm. Human data: none, but domain-knowledge cell features are hand-written in the strongest variant. Lesson: remembering and returning (avoiding "detachment" and "derailment") beats intrinsic motivation alone; it requires a resettable simulator in phase 1. This is the closest published template for a save-state Pokémon agent.

### 1.3 Self-play and populations at scale (2018-2022)

- **OpenAI Five (Dota 2)** - OpenAI, "Dota 2 with Large Scale Deep Reinforcement Learning," arXiv 1912.06680 (Dec 2019) [V]. Beat world champions Team OG on 13 Apr 2019, the first AI to beat the world champions at an esports game [V]. Algorithm: PPO with a large LSTM and self-play (the abstract does not name PPO; I take it from memory [M]). Compute: batches of ~2M frames every 2 seconds and about 10 months of continual training [V]. Human data: none; but it used hand-designed shaped rewards, a restricted hero pool, and "surgery" to carry weights across game-version and architecture changes [M]. Lesson: self-play + enough compute can solve a long-horizon (~20k-step) team game; continual training across changing code is itself an engineering contribution.
- **AlphaStar (StarCraft II)** - Vinyals et al., "Grandmaster level in StarCraft II using multi-agent reinforcement learning," *Nature* 575, Nov 2019 [V]. Grandmaster in all three races, ranked above 99.8% of active Battle.net players, under human-like camera and action-rate constraints [V]. Algorithm: supervised learning on human replays to initialize, then a league with main agents and exploiter agents trained by RL [V on the league and the mix of imitation/self-play; replay specifics from memory, M]. Compute: weeks on TPU pods [M, UNVERIFIED numbers]. Lesson: a human-data imitation prior plus a league that explicitly targets exploits is what made self-play robust in a game with strategic cycles.
- **Quake III Capture the Flag** - Jaderberg et al., *Science* 364, 859-865, 2019 [V] (arXiv 1807.01281, titled "Human-level performance in first-person multiplayer games with population-based deep reinforcement learning" [V]). From pixels and points only, a population of agents trained concurrently across thousands of parallel matches on randomly generated maps; each agent learns its own internal reward to supplement sparse win/loss; agents beat strong human players as opponents and as teammates [V]. No human data. Lesson: population-based training plus learned internal rewards plus procedural levels yields generalization to unseen maps.
- **Gran Turismo Sophy** - Wurman et al., "Outracing champion Gran Turismo drivers with deep reinforcement learning," *Nature* 602, 223-228, Feb 2022 [V]. Won head-to-head against four of the world's best GT drivers [V]. Algorithm: model-free deep RL (QR-SAC [M]) with mixed-scenario training and a reward designed to encode racing etiquette/sportsmanship [V]. Compute: a fleet of PlayStation consoles [M, UNVERIFIED size]. No human demonstrations [M]. Lesson: the hard part was reward and scenario design (what to penalize, which situations to oversample), not the optimizer. Hand-designed reward is a legitimate, published ingredient in a "superhuman" result.
- **Cicero (Diplomacy)** - Meta FAIR Diplomacy Team, "Human-level play in the game of Diplomacy by combining language models with strategic reasoning," *Science*, Nov 2022, DOI 10.1126/science.ade9097 [V]. Across 40 games in an anonymous online league it more than doubled the average human score and placed in the top 10% of players who played more than one game [V]. Algorithm: a dialogue language model conditioned on intents plus a planning/RL module whose policy is regularized toward human-like play [M: the regularization is piKL]. Trained with human game logs [M]. Lesson: in a game with cooperation, pure self-play drifts to conventions humans do not follow; regularize toward human data. Not a single-agent game, but the clearest early RL+LLM success.

### 1.4 Generalization benchmarks and procedural games (2018-2022)

- **Sonic Retro Contest** - Nichol et al., "Gotta Learn Fast: A New Benchmark for Generalization in RL," arXiv 1804.03720, OpenAI, Apr 2018 [V]. Train on a set of Sonic levels, evaluate on held-out levels; baselines (PPO, Rainbow, joint-training) generalize poorly [M]. Lesson: training-set levels are memorized; held-out levels expose it.
- **Procgen** - Cobbe et al., "Leveraging Procedural Generation to Benchmark Reinforcement Learning," ICML 2020 [V]. 16 procedurally generated games; diverse level distributions and larger models both improve sample efficiency and generalization [V].
- **Obstacle Tower** - Juliani et al. (Unity/NYU), IJCAI 2019 [V]. Procedurally generated 3D tower (up to 100 floors) scored on unseen instances; baselines were well below human [V abstract]. The 2019 challenge winner reportedly reached roughly floor 19 [UNVERIFIED]. Lesson: even "short per-floor" hierarchical tasks overwhelm vanilla deep RL when seeds are held out.
- **Crafter** - Hafner, "Benchmarking the Spectrum of Agent Capabilities," ICLR 2022 [V]. 2D Minecraft-like survival, 22 achievements, procedurally generated; designed to be trainable in 5M steps vs Procgen's 200M across 16 games [V]. Craftax (Matthews et al., ICML 2024) is a faster JAX successor [M]. Lesson: a cheap long-horizon "tech-tree" proxy; useful for prototyping before spending emulator hours.

### 1.5 Minecraft: the closest published analog to a gated tech tree (2022-2025)

- **MineDojo** - Fan et al., "MineDojo: Building Open-Ended Embodied Agents with Internet-Scale Knowledge," NeurIPS 2022 Datasets and Benchmarks, Outstanding Paper award in that track [V (award is D&B, not main track)]. 3k+ tasks plus an internet knowledge base (wiki pages, Reddit, ~300k hours of video per NVIDIA's blog) and MineCLIP, a video-language reward model [V, figures vary by outlet]. Lesson: language-conditioned reward from internet data as a reward-shaping substitute.
- **VPT (Video PreTraining)** - Baker et al., OpenAI, NeurIPS 2022 [V]. Train an inverse dynamics model on a small labeled contractor set, label a large pool of unlabeled web video, behavior-clone a foundation policy, then fine-tune with imitation and RL. Fine-tuned VPT crafts diamond pickaxes in 2.5% of 10-minute episodes, reported as the first agent to craft diamond tools; RL from scratch barely gets any reward and never learns to collect logs [V, OpenAI blog]. Human data: yes, large-scale (pretraining). Lesson: for a long-horizon game with sparse reward, a behavior prior is the difference between zero progress and meaningful progress; RL is the refinement stage.
- **DreamerV3** - Hafner et al., "Mastering diverse control tasks through world models," *Nature*, April 2025 [V] (arXiv 2301.04104). One hyperparameter config across 150+ tasks; to our knowledge the first algorithm to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch with no human data or curricula [V]. Time to first diamond is reported as ~30M env steps / 17 days of play on the project page; Gigazine says about 9 days, so check the Nature paper [V, conflict]. Caveat to verify: the Minecraft setup used faster block breaking and an adjusted action space [M, UNVERIFIED]. Lesson: a learned world model plus imagination handles sparse-reward, long-horizon exploration when the environment is visually regular.
- **Voyager** - Wang et al., arXiv 2305.16291, NVIDIA/Caltech et al., May 2023 [V]; I could not confirm a journal venue [V: no TMLR listing found]. GPT-4 black box; automatic curriculum, a growing library of executable code skills verified by environment feedback, iterative prompting with errors and self-verification [V]. Reported gains (e.g., ~3.3x more unique items, faster tech-tree milestones) come from a third-party summary [V, secondary]. No training; uses the Mineflayer API, not pixels. Lesson: code-as-skills with verification is a strong LLM pattern, but depends on a symbolic API.
- **JARVIS-1** - Wang et al., arXiv 2311.05997, Nov 2023; workshop version at NeurIPS 2023 [V]; I found no TPAMI listing. Multimodal LLM planning with a multimodal memory of past experience feeding goal-conditioned controllers; >200 tasks; 12.5% success on the long-horizon diamond-pickaxe task, ~5x prior reported [V via aggregator]. Lesson: memory of successes lets an LLM planner self-improve without weights.

### 1.6 NetHack: a long-horizon symbolic game that is still unbeaten (2020-2025)

- **NLE** - Küttler et al., "The NetHack Learning Environment," NeurIPS 2020 [V]. Procedurally generated, stochastic, extremely long episodes; open-sourced. Lesson: a deliberately unsolved benchmark.
- **NetHack Challenge (NeurIPS 2021)** - Hambro et al., "Insights from the NeurIPS 2021 NetHack Challenge," PMLR 176:41-52, 2022 [V]. Neural, symbolic, and hybrid entries compared; symbolic bots beat deep RL "by a large margin," several entries beat the previous best, none came close to ascending [V]. The winner was a hand-coded symbolic bot (AutoAscend) [M, UNVERIFIED name]. Lesson: handwritten domain knowledge beat learned policies on a long-horizon roguelike; the honest benchmark for "RL beats scripts" is not yet met.
- **NLD** - "Dungeons and Data: A Large-Scale NetHack Dataset," NeurIPS 2022 Datasets and Benchmarks [M; venue/authorship not confirmed, the dataset's existence is confirmed via the NLE PyPI page]. Enables imitation research on human and bot trajectories.
- **Scaling laws for imitation** - Tuyls et al., "Scaling Laws for Imitation Learning in Single-Agent Games," TMLR 2024 [V]. Imitation loss and mean return follow smooth power laws in compute on Atari and NetHack; the best NetHack agent is reported as 1.7x the prior offline state of the art (abstract; other mirrors say 1.5x, so the number changed between versions) [V, conflict]. Lesson: if a scaling-law regime holds for a game, data and model size become predictable levers; Pokémon has no equivalent corpus yet.
- **BALROG** - Paglieri et al., ICLR 2025 [V]. Benchmarks LLM/VLM agents on games including NetHack; models do moderately on easy games and struggle on hard ones; several do worse with visual input [V]. Games in the suite (BabyAI, Crafter, TextWorld, Baba Is AI, MiniHack, NetHack) are from memory [M]. Lesson: LLM agents reliably fail at long-horizon planning and vision-grounded decisions in games with real depth.
- **Motif / Maestromotif** (Klissarov et al., LLM preference-based intrinsic reward for NetHack, ICLR 2024 / 2025) [M, not re-checked]. LLM labels pairs of event captions; the preference becomes an intrinsic reward for RL. Mentioned because it is the closest published "LLM writes the reward, RL learns the policy" recipe on a long-horizon game.

### 1.7 Retro console games: Mario, Zelda, Sonic, Mega Man, and speedrun/TAS-style bots (2009-2025)

- **Mario AI Championship** (Togelius et al., 2009-2012) [M]: the winning entry was Robin Baumgarten's A* planner with a forward model [M]. Lesson: with a forward model, search beats learned policies on short platformers.
- **Tom Murphy's learnfun/playfun** - "The First Level of Super Mario Bros. is Easy with Lexicographic Orderings and Time Travel," SIGBOVIK 2013 [V]. Learns an objective function (lexicographic orderings on RAM locations) from a short human demonstration, then searches emulator inputs with save-state rewind. Played several NES games; weak when progress is non-monotone in memory [V on method; game list from memory]. Lesson: RAM-derived objectives plus emulator reset are old tricks and still the practical basis of Pokémon RAM-based reward.
- **MarI/O** - SethBling, 2015 [V via press coverage]. NEAT neuroevolution on Super Mario World level 1, fitness = rightward progress; roughly a day / 34 generations to finish the level; overfit to the level and needed retraining per level [V]. NEAT is Stanley & Miikkulainen 2002 [M]. Lesson: neuroevolution plus a progress fitness works for a single level but not for transfer.
- **Sonic** - see 1.4; the OpenAI Retro Contest is the standard held-out-level benchmark [V].
- **Legend of Zelda, Mega Man, Super Mario Bros. full clears** - RL "beats the game" claims here are mostly hobby repos and blog posts; I found no peer-reviewed full-game RL clear of Zelda or Mega Man [UNVERIFIED absence; not exhaustively searched]. PPO on Super Mario Bros. commonly solves early worlds and fails on later ones [M, UNVERIFIED]. LLM VLMs on Zelda: Link's Awakening, Kirby's Dream Land, Doom II etc. are in VideoGameBench (below), where frontier models barely get past openings [V].
- **TASBot / TAS community** [M]: human-authored tool-assisted speedruns replayed by robots. Not RL. Relevant because it provides the "optimal path with frame-perfect inputs" ground truth for Pokémon, and because the RAM maps/event-flag lists built by that community are the same assets RL projects use.

### 1.8 Sample efficiency: Atari 100k and world models (2019-2024)

- **Atari 100k** (Kaiser et al., SimPLe, 2019/ICLR 2020 [M]) fixes 100k agent steps (~2 hours of play). **EfficientZero** - Ye et al., "Mastering Atari Games with Limited Data," NeurIPS 2021 [V]: MuZero + self-supervised consistency + value-prefix + off-policy correction; 194.3% mean / 109.0% median human normalized on Atari 100k with ~2 hours of experience (secondary coverage quotes 190.4%/116.0%, so versions differ) [V, conflict]. **BBF** - Schwarzer et al., "Bigger, Better, Faster: Human-level Atari with human-level efficiency," ICML 2023 [V arXiv 2305.19452]: model-free, large network with resets and a higher update ratio, reaches human-level on Atari 100k; EfficientZero V2 later reports exceeding both [V]. Lesson: sample efficiency is a different axis from "beating the game"; none of this has been shown on a game needing >10^5 decisions per episode.

### 1.9 Generalist and foundation agents (2022-2026)

- **Gato** - Reed et al., "A Generalist Agent," TMLR Nov 2022 (arXiv 2205.06175) [V]. One transformer with one set of weights trained on 604 tasks (Atari, captioning, chat, real-robot stacking) [V]. Lesson: one network can imitate many embodiments, but per-task performance is mediocre and the work is behavior cloning from expert data (not RL for new games) [M on the latter].
- **Genie** - Bruce et al., "Genie: Generative Interactive Environments," ICML 2024 [V]. 11B parameter foundation world model trained on unlabeled internet video with a latent-action model; users can act frame by frame [V]. **Genie 3** (Aug 2025) generates interactive 720p / 24 fps worlds from text with several minutes of consistency, per press coverage; I did not open the DeepMind post [V, secondary]. Lesson: generative simulators could supply unlimited training worlds, but no shown use for beating an existing fixed game.
- **SIMA** - SIMA Team, DeepMind, "Scaling Instructable Agents Across Many Simulated Worlds," arXiv 2404.10179 (Mar 2024, v3 Oct 2024) [V]. Language-instructed keyboard/mouse agents over commercial 3D games and research environments; early zero-shot transfer of basic skills, many tasks out of reach [V]. **SIMA 2** (Nov 2025) is Gemini-based, reportedly 65% success on known games vs 31% for SIMA 1, with Gemini generating tasks and rewards for self-improvement; also tested in Genie 3-generated worlds [V, press coverage only]. Lesson: instruction-following over a skill library, not completion of single games; short-horizon tasks.
- **Multi-game Decision Transformer** (Lee et al., NeurIPS 2022) and **Cradle** (Tan et al. 2024, general computer control, Red Dead Redemption II) [M, UNVERIFIED]. Mentioned for completeness; not verified.

### 1.10 Pokémon-specific RL (2023-2026)

- **PokemonRedExperiments / "pokerl"** - Peter Whidden and collaborators; open-source PPO-from-pixels (plus RAM-derived exploration/event rewards) project with a popular YouTube video (>1M views per a secondary source); the repository (PWhiddy/PokemonRedExperiments) has ~7.8k GitHub stars [V, secondary]. A later PufferLib-based fork (`pokemonred_puffer`) and the "Pokemon RL Edition" book describe a full-game clear by a <10M-parameter policy with substantial caveats (scripts, swarming, map-ID rewards, infinite money; not stable with all scripts off) per `../papers/neurips2025-pokemon-red-rl.md` [from that note; not independently re-verified this session]. Not peer reviewed. Lesson: the full-game RL claim exists but is entangled with heavy human assistance.
- **Pleines et al.** - "Pokemon Red via Reinforcement Learning," arXiv 2502.19920 (27 Feb 2025; v2 11 Mar 2025) [V]. A simple environment and PPO baseline that completes "the first several hours" up to Cerulean City; ablations show reward-shaping vulnerabilities (agents exploit specific signals) [V]. The "NeurIPS 2025" attribution is not supported; one secondary source says IEEE CoG 2025 [see companion note; UNVERIFIED]. Lesson: a clean academic baseline stops at ~20% of the game.
- **PokeRL** - Mudireddy & Patibandla, arXiv 2604.10812, Apr 2026 [V]. Loop-aware PyBoy wrapper with map masking, multi-layer anti-loop/anti-spam, and a dense hierarchical reward, scoped to leaving the house, reaching tall grass, and winning the first rival battle; says PPO is brittle and degenerates into action loops, menu spam, and unproductive wandering [V]. Lesson: even the first hour needs engineering, and the failure modes mirror LLM-agent loops.
- **PokeAgent Challenge** - Karten et al., "The PokéAgent Challenge: Competitive and Long-Context Learning at Scale," NeurIPS 2025 competition track; arXiv 2603.15563 (Mar 2026) [V]. Two tracks: Showdown battling (dataset of >20M trajectories, baselines) and RPG speedrunning (standardized evaluation, multi-agent orchestration). 100+ teams. Findings: large gaps between LLM generalists, RL specialists, and elite humans, and Pokémon battling is "nearly orthogonal" to standard LLM benchmarks [V]. Speedrun track details (Emerald to the first gym, winner is an LLM-scripted-policy-distilled-to-RL entry) are from `llm-agents-pokemon.md` [not re-verified here].
- **Metamon** - Grigsby, Xie, Sasek, Zheng, Zhu, "Human-Level Competitive Pokémon via Scalable Offline Reinforcement Learning with Transformers," Reinforcement Learning Journal / RLC 2025 (arXiv 2504.04395) [V]. Reconstructs first-person trajectories from spectator logs spanning more than a decade; trains transformers by imitation, then offline RL, then offline fine-tuning on self-play; no explicit search; top 10% of active human players in online play [V]. Lesson: the battle subgame is solved to a human-expert level by offline RL on human logs. A trainer-battle module for Crystal could be built from this recipe.

### 1.11 LLM game agents and benchmarks (2024-2026)

- **BALROG** (ICLR 2025) [V] - see 1.6.
- **lmgame-Bench** - Hu et al. (UCSD / MBZUAI / Berkeley), "lmgame-Bench: How Good are LLMs at Playing Games?," arXiv 2505.15146, ICLR 2026 [V]. Six games (platformer, puzzle, narrative) behind a Gym API with a modular harness that can be toggled to isolate perception, memory, and reflection; 13 models evaluated; finds bottlenecks in visual state extraction, reflection, spatiotemporal reasoning, long-context reasoning; reports RL on one game transfers to unseen games and planning tasks [V]. Lesson: harness components change what you are measuring.
- **VideoGameBench** - Zhang, Griffiths, Narasimhan, Press (Princeton), arXiv 2505.18134, May 2025 [V]. 10 1990s games (Game Boy / Game Boy Color / MS-DOS) from raw frames, real time; three held out. Best models (Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude 3.7 Sonnet) complete 0.48% overall and 1.6% of the Lite (paused-while-thinking) setting [V]. Lesson: with no game-specific scaffolding, frontier VLMs do not get past openings.
- **Orak** - Park et al. (KRAFTON/SNU/NVIDIA/UW-Madison), arXiv 2506.03610, ICLR 2026 [V]. 12 games across genres through an MCP interface; leaderboards, arenas, ablations of agentic modules and fine-tuning, plus a released expert-trajectory dataset [V]. Pokémon Red is scoped to beating Brock (per `llm-agents-pokemon.md`, GPT-5 88.9 vs human novice 86.1) [not re-verified]. Lesson: one-gym scope is already within LLM reach with text state; the full game is not what Orak measures.
- **Livestream agents** (Claude/Gemini/GPT plays Pokémon) are covered in `llm-agents-pokemon.md`. In short: Gemini on Blue and Crystal, GPT on Red/Crystal/Emerald, Claude on Red (reported complete in 2026) with harnesses that read RAM into text, expose pathfinding, and keep notes [community-reported; not peer-reviewed]. Their lesson is the harness (memory, A*, critic) matters more than the base model, and loops are the dominant failure.

## 2. Synthesis

### (a) A taxonomy of what "beating a game" has meant

| # | Meaning | Examples | What it hides |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Score above a human baseline | Agent57 (57 Atari), MuZero | Averages over short-horizon games; unsolved ones are skipped for years |
| 2 | Beating human opponents / champions | OpenAI Five, AlphaStar, Quake CTF, GT Sophy, Cicero | Restricted rules (hero pool, APM caps, curated cars/tracks) |
| 3 | A milestone in a tech tree | Minecraft diamond (DreamerV3, VPT 2.5% diamond pickaxe, JARVIS-1 12.5%) | A milestone is not a full game; success rates are per short episode |
| 4 | Reaching the credits / ending | Pokémon Red/Blue/Crystal LLM livestreams, pokerl (with scripts) | Step counting is inconsistent; harnesses carry large human assistance |
| 5 | Speedrun / optimality | TAS bots, learnfun/playfun, PokeAgent speedrun track | Optimizes time, not robustness |
| 6 | Generalization to held-out levels or games | Procgen, Obstacle Tower, Sonic Retro, Crafter, SIMA | Often shorter horizons per level |
| 7 | Fraction of benchmark progress | BALROG, VideoGameBench, lmgame-Bench, Orak | Progress metrics are benchmark-specific |
| 8 | "Beats the game" via search with a forward model | Mario A* agents, Murphy's playfun, Go-Explore phase 1 | Uses simulator reset/clone, which a deployed agent lacks |

Cross-cutting axis: **assistance level.** Orthogonal to meaning, every result sits somewhere on: privileged state (RAM/API vs pixels), human data (none, demos, large corpus), hand-shaped reward, scripts/macros, simulator reset/save-state, action abstraction, and compute. A defensible Crystal claim must state its position on each.

### (b) Recurring ingredients that worked

1. **Self-play / league / population.** OpenAI Five, AlphaStar, Quake CTF, GT Sophy. Needed when the opponent is the environment; not applicable to a single-player RPG except battles (Metamon fine-tunes on self-play).
2. **Curriculum / procedural diversity.** Quake CTF random maps, GT Sophy mixed scenarios, Procgen, Obstacle Tower, Voyager's automatic curriculum. Diversity prevents memorization of a single path; a single-game story is the opposite regime.
3. **Imitation priors from human data.** AlphaStar's replays, VPT's inverse-dynamics labeling, Cicero's human logs, Metamon's spectator-log reconstruction, Tuyls scaling laws. Strongest correlation with progress on sparse-reward long-horizon tasks; RL alone almost never solves them (VPT: from-scratch RL never learns logs).
4. **Reset-to-state / return-then-explore.** Go-Explore, Salimans & Chen, Murphy's rewind search, Atari 100k-adjacent tricks. Turns long-horizon exploration into many short problems; the main shortcut for emulated games.
5. **Huge compute.** OpenAI Five (~10 months, millions of frames per second of experience), Agent57 (tens of billions of frames), DreamerV3 (~tens of millions of steps for diamonds). Compute buys what priors don't, but cost grows sharply with horizon.
6. **Intrinsic motivation and adaptive exploration.** RND, NGU, Agent57's meta-controller. Necessary but insufficient at Montezuma scale; typically combined with reset or demos.
7. **Reward design.** GT Sophy etiquette, OpenAI Five shaping, Quake CTF learned internal reward. Often undersold in headlines; Pokémon work (Pleines, PokeRL) shows shaped reward is also the main source of exploits.
8. **World models and planning.** MuZero, EfficientZero, DreamerV3. Sample efficiency and imagination for sparse reward; unproven for text-heavy GBC screens [UNVERIFIED].
9. **Structured state instead of pixels.** Voyager (API), Murphy (RAM), pokerl (RAM events), LLM Pokémon harnesses (RAM-to-text). Repeatedly the largest practical lever; pure-pixel LLM runs fail (VideoGameBench).
10. **LLM as planner/reward/skill author, with verifiable feedback.** Voyager (verified code skills), Motif (preference reward), JARVIS-1 (memory), Heatz-style scripted-policy distillation in PokeAgent [see companion note]. Works where a verifier (RAM predicate, game state) exists.

### (c) Where Pokémon-style long-horizon RPGs sit, and what hasn't been done

**Position.** Compared with the solved set: Atari games last minutes and are mostly score-driven; Dota/StarCraft are ~20k-30k decisions but have dense in-game feedback and self-play; Minecraft diamond is ~10^4-10^5 decisions with a clean linear tech tree. A full Crystal playthrough is on the order of 10^5-10^6 agent steps (livestream runs of Red used roughly 10^4-2x10^5 LLM turns depending on harness, per the companion note), with sparse story milestones, gating by items/HMs/flags, text/menu-driven dialogue, irreversible missables, and a combinatorial battle subgame. It resembles Montezuma's Revenge (key-gated rooms) at 100x scale, and NetHack (long, partially observable, knowledge-heavy) without death as the limiter.

**What hasn't been done (to my knowledge; absence claims are [UNVERIFIED])**
- A peer-reviewed RL agent that clears a Pokémon main game with scripts off and a published ablation of which human priors were necessary.
- Any published RL agent (rather than an LLM harness) for Pokémon Crystal specifically.
- A Go-Explore-style archive built on event-flag/RAM cells for a story RPG, with compute-to-clear numbers.
- A VPT-like behavior prior for a Game Boy RPG, trained on the (now large) corpus of public LLM and human playthrough logs.
- A joint overworld + battle agent: Metamon-quality battles inside an overworld policy.
- Standardized full-game metrics: steps, wall-clock, dollars, tokens, scripts used, seeds, and generalization across Red/Blue/Yellow/Gold/Silver/Crystal.
- Evidence on which gates (HM acquisition, puzzles, ledge/NPC blocks) dominate total cost, beyond anecdotes.
- Cost-aware LLM+RL hybrids that report a Pareto frontier (calls/tokens/dollars vs milestones), as the trigger-based design of this repo proposes.

### (d) Six research opportunities that follow

1. **Resettable archive for a story RPG.** Implement Go-Explore on Crystal with cells defined by (map, coarse position, event-flag/badge/HM subset) via PyBoy save-states, then robustify with a backward-algorithm policy. Ablate cell definitions and report steps-to-clear. Fills the gap between Montezuma-scale archives and 10^6-step games; directly answers "can RL do it without scripts, given reset?"
2. **Verifiable-reward LLM hybrid with a cost frontier.** Use the LLM as sparse supervisor that proposes subgoals/reward code checked by RAM predicates (Motif/Voyager-style), trigger-gated to minimize calls. Report milestone-vs-dollar Pareto curves against LLM-every-step and pure-PPO baselines. This is the repo's own thesis and is publishable if the accounting is rigorous.
3. **Offline pretraining on public playthrough logs.** Build a Crystal behavior prior (VPT/IDM style) from human streams and LLM-agent logs, then RL fine-tune; fit a Tuyls-style scaling curve for imitation on Pokémon. Tests whether Pokémon behaves like NetHack/Minecraft in the scaling regime.
4. **Battle module via offline RL.** Port the Metamon recipe (spectator-log reconstruction, transformer, offline-then-self-play) to Gen 2 trainer battles and plug it into the overworld agent; measure how much of the full-game failure rate is battle vs navigation vs puzzle.
5. **A standardized full-game benchmark and "scripts ladder."** Define a milestone ladder with RAM-verified checkpoints, normalized step/time/cost accounting, and a graded list of allowed assists (pixels vs RAM, reset, macros, shaped reward). Evaluate scripts-off generalization across Red, Gold/Silver, and Crystal, plus seed-robustness. Addresses the metric-hygiene problem (inconsistent steps/turns) documented in `llm-agents-pokemon.md`.
6. **Gate-aware curriculum and world-model testing.** Automatically build the dependency graph of gates (Cut, Surf, Strength, keys, badges) from RAM flags, train goal-conditioned skills over it, and test a DreamerV3-style world model on GBC text/menu screens for imagined exploration. Quantifies where cost concentrates (puzzle gadgets like boulders, spinners, shutters) and whether learned skills transfer across maps.
